"How much does a website cost?" is the first question almost every client asks, and the most common answer, "it depends", is true but not helpful. This guide breaks the price down into the things it actually depends on, explains the running costs people forget, and shows our own prices so you have a real reference point.

## The short answer

For a small or medium business, a professionally built website usually falls into one of four bands:

1. **Do-it-yourself builders** (Wix, Squarespace and similar): a monthly subscription and your own time. Cheapest to start, limited design and performance.
2. **Freelancer or template build:** a one-off fee to set up and customise an existing theme, plus ongoing hosting and plugin costs.
3. **Custom build by a studio or agency:** a one-off fee for a site designed and coded for your business, plus modest hosting.
4. **Enterprise build:** large teams, long timelines, complex integrations and ongoing retainers.

At Matterpixel, a custom, mobile-first business website starts from **$700**, and a site with built-in lead capture and automation starts from **$1,200**. The rest of this guide explains what moves a project up or down within any of these bands.

## What actually drives the cost

### 1. Number of unique page designs

Price follows unique templates more than total pages. A site with a home page, a service page template used for eight services, an about page and a contact page is four designs, not eleven pages. Every distinct layout adds design and build time.

### 2. Custom design versus a theme

A theme is cheaper because the design work is already done, but you inherit its structure, its code weight and everyone else's look. A custom design costs more upfront and fits your brand, your content and your customers exactly.

### 3. Content

Who writes the words and who provides the images? Copywriting, product photography and video are often the most underestimated costs. If your content is ready, the project is faster and cheaper. If it needs to be created, budget for it separately.

### 4. Functionality

Each feature beyond pages of content adds cost:

- Contact and quote forms (simple)
- Booking and scheduling with reminders
- Online payments or a shop
- Customer accounts and logins
- Integrations with a CRM, accounting or inventory tool
- AI chat or WhatsApp automation
- Multiple languages

### 5. Performance and SEO

A site that genuinely scores 90+ on Google PageSpeed for mobile, with proper technical SEO, takes more care than one that merely looks finished. It is also what makes the site earn its cost back, as we explain in [Why page speed sells](/insights/why-pagespeed-sells).

### 6. Timeline

Rushed projects cost more because they need more people working in parallel. A realistic timeline is usually the cheapest one.

## Running costs people forget

The build is not the whole price. Plan for:

- **Domain name:** a small yearly fee
- **Hosting:** from a few dollars to tens of dollars a month for a typical business site
- **Business email:** usually charged per user per month
- **Premium plugins or theme licences:** common on WordPress, often renewed yearly
- **Maintenance and security updates:** either your time, or a support plan
- **Content updates and improvements:** new pages, offers and seasonal changes

A cheap build with expensive plugins and constant maintenance can cost more over three years than a well-built custom site. Compare the total cost over the life of the site, not just the first invoice.

## What a fair quote should include

Whoever you hire, a professional quote should state clearly:

- The pages and templates included
- Every feature and integration, with what is out of scope
- Who provides content, and what happens if it is late
- The number of design revision rounds
- Performance and SEO standards, ideally as measurable targets
- Payment terms (we work 50% upfront, 50% on delivery)
- Who owns the code, design files and content at the end (it should be you)
- What support is included after launch, and what it costs after that

If a quote is vague on any of these, ask. Vague quotes are where budgets grow.

## Red flags

- **No written scope.** "We'll build you a website" is not a scope.
- **You do not own the result.** Some providers keep ownership of the site or lock you into their platform.
- **Very low price with high monthly fees.** The cost is simply moved into a long contract.
- **No performance commitment.** A site that looks good but loads slowly on phones will cost you customers.
- **No portfolio you can actually click through.** Screenshots are easy. Live sites are proof.

## Is a cheap website ever the right choice?

Yes. If you are testing a new idea, need a simple online presence quickly, or have almost no budget, a DIY builder is a sensible start. You can move to a custom site once the business is proven. The mistake is paying for a mid-priced site that has the limitations of a cheap one.
